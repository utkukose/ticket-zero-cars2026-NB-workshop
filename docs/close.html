Wrap-up

Anything an AI assistant reads is untrusted input.

Security has to come from the architecture around the model, not from asking the model to behave. That is the one idea to carry out of this hour.

For everyone

What never to put in a chat window

This reaches past builders. Anything typed into an AI tool may be read, logged, or acted on by systems and people you did not intend. The habit that protects you is the same one that stops phishing.

Keep out
Passwords, card and account numbers, one-time codes, and anything you would not post publicly.
Not yours to share
Other people's personal data, and confidential or internal documents, pasted in for help.
The phishing link
Prompt injection and phishing share a root: a confident, official tone is not proof of authority, for a person or for an AI.

Where this leads

Careers in AI security

The skills from this hour point to real and growing roles. A strong grasp of how systems handle untrusted input, plus hands-on practice, prepares you for all of them. IEEE membership and student chapters are a direct way to build the network and the practice.

Security engineering
Build the controls you armed in Part 3 into real systems.
Red-teaming

Privacy

Incident response
Handle breaches when controls fail.
Governance
Write the rules that require the controls in the first place.
